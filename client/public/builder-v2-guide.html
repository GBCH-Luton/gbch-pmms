Locked Job Focus Mode

A proposal to stop builders forgetting to clock in/out and logging jobs after the fact, by locking the app down to one job at a time with a big running timer and a single, mandatory "why did you stop" step.

🎯 The problem this solves

Managers report builders often forget to clock in or out, and don't log jobs in real time — they log them after finishing, which makes durations and location data unreliable.

🧱 Already built (being reused)

Daily "Clock In for the Day" gate, per-job Start/Pause/Resume/Complete, Hold reasons + note, "Couldn't get access", and a 17:00 clock-out reminder to the builder all already exist — this proposal restructures and tightens them, it doesn't start from zero.

Existing

🆕 What's actually new

Full-screen lockdown while a job is active, one unified Stop button, a big live timer, a new "Unable to Do Job" manager metric, and a real auto-clock-out (not just a reminder).

New

⚠️ Needs a decision first

Four points are decided already (auto-clock-out as a last resort, Unable-to-Do staying manager-actioned, the materials-wait split, and the menu staying reachable while locked). One is still open — see the Open Questions tab.

9 End-of-day clock-out escalation

Today, a builder still clocked in past 17:00 gets one reminder push and nothing else happens — the shift stays open until a manager manually closes it, however long that takes. This proposal keeps that manual path exactly as it is, and adds a safety net on top:

  • The manager gets told too, not just the builder — and can act on it themselves, same as today.
  • Only if both of them ignore it past a configurable grace period does the system step in and close the shift itself — backdated to the 17:00 deadline, not whenever the auto-close actually ran.

Found while checking this proposal against what's already in PMMS today. Three are now decided; two are still open.

✅ Decided

Auto clock-out is a last resort, not a replacement

The existing rule that a manager has to manually close a shift stays exactly as it is — the builder's own clock-out and the manager's manual close both still work like today. The system only steps in on its own if both of them ignore it past the grace period, so it doesn't actually contradict the earlier "no automatic clock-out" ruling — it's a safety net behind the human paths, not instead of them.

✅ Decided

"Unable to Do the Job" stays manager-actioned, not automatic

It gets its own separate metric on the dashboard (not folded into a general On Hold count) specifically so a manager sees it and reassigns it themselves — no automatic unassignment.

✅ Decided

A multi-day materials wait doesn't lock the builder idle

"Waiting for Materials" now asks which kind it is (step 5): ordered/delivered means the job goes On Hold and releases the builder to work on something else while they wait; going to fetch it themselves is a short, timed personal trip that stays locked, exactly like a lunch break. Getting that second case timed accurately — instead of guessed at afterwards — is the actual point of this whole proposal.

✅ Decided

The menu stays reachable — job screens don't

The header and ☰ menu stay accessible even while a job or a personal-trip timer is locked — Team Chat, Metrics, Mileage, notifications all still work. What stays hidden is anything job-related: the job list, Available Jobs, raising a new ticket. So there's always a way to reach a manager or check something, without being able to wander off to another job.

Question 1

One grace period for everyone?

Drafted as a single global setting (default 2 hours). Does it need to vary by division or role, or is one number for the whole company fine to start with?
