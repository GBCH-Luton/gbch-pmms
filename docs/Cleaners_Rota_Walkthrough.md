# Cleaners Rota — Live Walkthrough

A step-by-step demonstration of how the Cleaners Rota works in the
system, end to end. Follow this in order using two logins side by side
— your own Housekeeping Manager account, and a Housekeeper (cleaner)
account — to show the whole process live.

This walkthrough proves out every part of the process as originally
agreed:

- One cleaner responsible for each property, matched by gender
  requirement where one applies
- The routine two-week visit appearing automatically on the cleaner's
  own job list
- A cleaner reporting a delay, and that delay needing manager approval
  before it's accepted — with the job simply staying open the whole time
- A checklist as the baseline for every routine visit, with photos and
  notes as the supporting evidence on top
- Sign-off, exactly like a repair job
- Urgent cleaning jobs, raised and assigned the same way as any job
- A single Housekeeping page giving a manager the full picture: what's
  overdue, who's carrying what, and what needs approval

---

## Before you start

You'll need:
- Your own login, with the **Housekeeping Manager** role assigned
  (Settings/Admin → Access)
- A second login with the **Housekeeper** role, to demonstrate the
  cleaner's side (a test account works fine for this)

---

## Step 1 — Assign a cleaner to a property

**As the manager:**
1. Go to **Properties**, open any property, and go to its **Core**
   details tab
2. Find **"Assigned Cleaner"** and click **Assign**
3. Pick the cleaner from the list and **Save**

**What this shows:** properties are matched to one responsible cleaner,
who a manager can change at any time. If the property has a gender
requirement set (Restrictions tab), only cleaners who match that
requirement are offered in the list — nobody has to check this by hand.

---

## Step 2 — The routine visit appears automatically

Every property needs a visit at least once every two weeks. The system
watches this on its own and creates the job automatically once a
property is due — no manager action needed.

**As the cleaner:** open the job list — the routine visit for that
property is sitting there, the same way a repair job would be.

**What this shows:** the two-week cadence runs itself. A manager never
has to remember to chase this.

---

## Step 3 — Reporting a delay

Sometimes a cleaner can't get to a property in time.

**As the cleaner:**
1. Open the routine visit job (don't start it yet)
2. Tap **"Can't do this on time? Report a delay"**
3. Pick a reason, add a note if useful, and submit

**What this shows:** the reason is recorded straight away, but it is
**not** automatically accepted — the job doesn't disappear, close, or
change in any way. It's simply flagged for a manager to look at.

---

## Step 4 — Approving the delay

**As the manager:**
1. Go to the **Housekeeping** page
2. Under **"Pending Delay Reasons"**, find the job and read the
   cleaner's reason
3. Click **Approve** (or **Reject**, if the reason isn't good enough)

**What this shows:** nothing is accepted without a manager's sign-off.
Once approved, the job carries on exactly as before — it simply stays
open until the cleaner actually gets it done. The cleaner is notified
either way.

---

## Step 5 — Completing the visit: checklist first, evidence second

**As the cleaner:**
1. Start the job (**"I've arrived — start work"**)
2. Tap **"Mark complete"**
3. A checklist appears — try leaving one item unticked and notice the
   **Confirm** button stays disabled
4. Tick every item, add a note describing the work, and a photo if you
   have one
5. Confirm

**What this shows:** the checklist is the real baseline for a visit
being "done" — a cleaner physically cannot skip it. Photos and notes
sit on top of that as supporting evidence, exactly as agreed — not a
replacement for the checklist.

---

## Step 6 — Sign-off

**As the manager:**
1. Go to **Sign-Off**
2. Find the completed visit — you'll see the checklist results
   alongside the note and any photo
3. Click **Verify & Archive**

**What this shows:** a routine visit is reviewed and closed out through
exactly the same office process already used for repair jobs — nothing
new to learn.

---

## Step 7 — Raising an urgent clean

**As the manager:**
1. Go to **Log a Ticket**
2. Choose the property, then category **"Cleaning Rota"** → **"Urgent
   Clean"**
3. When picking who to assign it to, notice only Housekeeping staff are
   offered — not the maintenance team
4. Assign it and submit

**What this shows:** urgent cleaning jobs use the exact same
raise-a-job process as any repair — no separate system to learn — and
the system already knows to only offer cleaning staff for a cleaning
job.

---

## Step 8 — The Housekeeping overview

**As the manager**, go to the **Housekeeping** page and look at the
three sections:

- **Routine Visits Due** — every property with a cleaner assigned, most
  overdue first, colour-coded Overdue / Due Soon / OK
- **Cleaner Workload** — how many properties and open jobs each cleaner
  currently has, plus whether they're available, on leave, or off sick
- **Pending Delay Reasons** — anything waiting for a decision

**What this shows:** everything a manager needs to run the rota day to
day is in one place — what's coming up, who's carrying what, and what
needs a decision.

---

## What's not built yet

One thing was deliberately left out of this version: the system
doesn't yet **suggest** the best cleaner for a new or urgent job by
workload and area — a manager makes that call fully by hand for now, by
looking at the Workload section above. This was flagged from the start
as a "nice to have for later," not part of the core process.
